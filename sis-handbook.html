Modern Student Information System

A research-backed technical handbook for designing, building, and auditing a production-ready SIS for a Philippine college or university — compiled as reference material for the Akademus SIS project and for AI coding assistants working on it.

Part I · Executive Summary

What was researched, why it matters, and the overall recommended direction.

This handbook researched how mature Student Information Systems are designed and operated — drawing on vendor documentation (Ellucian Banner, Oracle PeopleSoft Campus Solutions, Workday Student, Anthology Student, Jenzabar, openSIS and others), software-architecture literature, database-design research, OWASP/NIST/ISO 27001 security guidance, university timetabling research, and Philippine data-privacy and higher-education regulation (RA 10173, the National Privacy Commission, CHED). The goal was to determine — from evidence, not assumption — how a real, secure, scalable, auditable SIS should be built.

Major findings
  • Mature SIS platforms converge on the same lifecycle and module set regardless of vendor: identity/curriculum/enrollment/scheduling/grading/finance/documents/audit, glued together with strict role-based permissions.
  • A modular monolith — not microservices — is the evidence-backed starting architecture for a single-institution SIS; both Twilio Segment and Amazon Prime Video migrated away from microservices back toward consolidated services, citing cost and complexity.
  • Curriculum and student records must be versioned and append-only; overwriting them is the single most common cause of corrupted academic history in real deployments.
  • University timetabling is a proven NP-hard problem; full AI/genetic-algorithm optimization is real but is an enhancement, not a Day-1 requirement — assisted, constraint-checked scheduling is the practical baseline.
  • Grades and academic records are Sensitive Personal Information under the Philippine Data Privacy Act (RA 10173) — this converts RBAC, encryption, and audit logging from “nice to have” into legal necessities.
Major risks identified
  • Broken access control remains the #1 real-world web risk (OWASP Top 10) — the majority of SIS-shaped systems fail here first.
  • Hard-deleting or overwriting historical academic records silently corrupts transcripts and degree audits with no recovery path.
  • Premature microservices adoption increases cost and failure surface without a matching increase in reliability for a single-institution system.
  • Un-tested backups and undefined RPO/RTO turn a routine incident into an unrecoverable one.
  • Undefined source-of-truth boundaries between Admissions, SIS, LMS, and Accounting create silently conflicting records.
Overall recommended direction

Build Akademus SIS as a modular monolith with explicit domain boundaries; make the student-identity model, curriculum versioning, and record-immutability decisions correctly from Phase 1 because they are the most expensive to reverse; enforce deny-by-default RBAC with full audit logging; treat scheduling and sectioning as assisted-not-fully-automatic workflows; and delegate heavy accounting to a dedicated finance system reconciled against SIS-owned enrollment and assessment data.

Part III · Literature & Industry Review

What existing SIS platforms reveal, beyond their marketing pages.

RESEARCHPer Ellucian's own materials, cited by Inside Higher Ed (2019), "more than 1,400 institutions worldwide use Banner to manage student grades, staff payrolls, course schedules, admissions and student financial aid" — deep functional coverage, but architecturally fragmented into separate student/financial/HR modules bolted together over decades. Workday Student is cloud-native with a unified data model shared with HCM/Financials (launched 2019; 18–36 month implementations). Oracle PeopleSoft Campus Solutions is metadata-driven (PeopleTools) with 30+ years of functional depth and an Integration Broker for external systems. Anthology Student and Jenzabar One offer configurable degree-audit modules within a modular ERP. Open-source options — Fedena (Ruby on Rails, 50+ modules) and openSIS (PHP/MySQL, K-12-leaning) — show the same core module set implemented at far smaller scale.

Analysis

Despite wildly different tech stacks and eras, every mature platform converges on the same pattern: a versioned curriculum, an enrollment engine gated by prerequisite/capacity rules, a locked-grade workflow, role-scoped portals, and an audit trail. This convergence — not any single vendor's implementation — is the strongest evidence for what Akademus SIS's module boundaries should look like.

Part IV · SIS Module Research

Purpose, users, workflow, rules, and best practice for each core module. Expand any module for detail.

1–4 · Authentication & Authorization CORE

Purpose: gate every read/write by identity and role. Users: everyone. Workflow: SSO/OIDC login → role + scope resolved → every request re-checked server-side.

Business rules: deny-by-default; a chairperson's approval scope is limited to their own department; an adviser sees only their advisees.

STANDARDFollow the OWASP Authorization and Authentication Cheat Sheets and NIST SP 800-63B for password/session handling. Common mistake: checking roles only in the frontend — trivially bypassed.

Student Records & Admissions Integration CORE

Purpose: the immutable core identity plus mutable current-state fields (contact info, current program). Data: identity, admission record, program history, curriculum assignment. Rule: student identity is created once from an "ApplicantAccepted" event and never duplicated — dedupe at admission time, not after.

Academic Structure, Curriculum & Program Management CORE

Purpose: colleges → departments → programs → versioned curricula → subjects. Rule: a curriculum change never edits an existing version — it creates a new one, so historical enrollment stays linked to the curriculum version under which it actually occurred. Full detail in Part V.

Academic Year, Semester & Section Management

Common mistake: hardcoding the "current" academic year in code — breaks every new term and forces annual code deploys. Terms and sections must be configurable data, carrying status (planning/open/active/grading/closed/archived).

Section creation: should be system-suggested, human-approved — e.g. 65 students at a 30-seat cap → system proposes Sections A(30)/B(30)/C(5) and flags C as under minimum for a merge decision.

Enrollment & Registration CORE

See full workflow research in Part VI.

Scheduling, Room Management & Faculty Assignment

See full constraint research in Part VII. Faculty assignment default: chairperson/department assigns (enforces expertise-matching and workload balance); faculty self-claiming is an optional layer within chair-approved limits.

Attendance, Grading & Academic Progress

See Part VIII.

Billing & Payments, Scholarships & Discounts

Recommendation: the SIS owns tuition assessment tied to enrollment; heavy accounting (general ledger, official receipts, refunds) belongs in a dedicated accounting/ERP system, reconciled nightly against SIS enrollment records. The SIS should never store raw card data — route payments through a gateway.

Portals — Student, Faculty, Registrar, Chairperson

Each is a role-scoped view over the same data, not a separate system. See UX guidance in Part XV.

Document Management & Clearance

STANDARDFollow the OWASP File Upload Cheat Sheet: allow-list file types, validate by magic bytes (not extension), store outside the webroot with no execute permission, AV-scan before acceptance, and authorize every download individually to prevent IDOR. Documents (transcripts, IDs, transfer credentials) contain Sensitive Personal Information — encrypt at rest.

Clearance is a multi-department sign-off gate (library, accounting, lab, guidance) blocking enrollment/graduation until all departments approve.

Notifications, Reports & Analytics

Event-driven, queued, multi-channel (in-app/email/SMS/push). Large reports and bulk notifications must run as background jobs, never inside the HTTP request that triggered them.

Graduation Management

Automated eligibility determination (degree audit + credits + documents + clearance) with mandatory registrar verification before conferral. Under CHED's Manual of Regulations for Private Higher Education (CMO No. 40 s.2008), Sec. 102 conditions release of credentials on settlement of accountabilities — model clearance as a hard gate before graduation.

Audit Logging & System Configuration CORE

See Part X. Configuration (terms, grading schemes, fee tables, honors thresholds) must be data, not code, so institutional policy changes don't require a redeploy.

APIs & Integration

See Part XIII for source-of-truth boundaries between Admissions, SIS, LMS, and Accounting.

Part V · Curriculum & Academic Records Research

Why curriculum versioning is one of the hardest decisions to retrofit.

RESEARCHDegree-audit platforms like Stellic explicitly model how existing credits map onto new program requirements when a student changes majors or when requirements change — this mapping only works if each curriculum revision is a distinct, addressable version rather than an in-place edit.

Recommendation

Model Program → CurriculumVersion → CurriculumSubject so "BS Computer Science — Curriculum 2024" and "BS Computer Science — Curriculum 2026" are fully independent, immutable snapshots. Each student is assigned to one specific version at admission. When requirements change, create a new version; existing students remain on their original version unless formally migrated through an adviser-approved equivalency mapping, itself audited.

ANALYSISEditing an existing curriculum record in place is the single most common cause of corrupted historical data in SIS deployments: a subject removed from "the curriculum" silently orphans every past student's completed-subject record, breaking degree audits retroactively. General-education requirement blocks should follow CHED CMO No. 20, s.2013 (roughly 36 units / 8 core GE courses) as a reusable block referenced by every program's curriculum version, not duplicated per program.

Part VII · Scheduling Research

RESEARCHThe University Course Timetabling Problem is a well-studied, combinatorial NP-hard problem, commonly modeled as a Constraint Satisfaction Problem and solved with genetic algorithms, integer programming, simulated annealing, or hybrids of these (multiple ScienceDirect / arXiv studies on constraint-based and GA-based university timetabling).

Hard constraints (never violated)Soft constraints (optimized where possible)
Faculty cannot teach two classes at onceFaculty's preferred teaching hours
Students cannot have overlapping required classesMinimized student idle time between classes
Rooms cannot host two classes at onceMinimized room changes
Lab subjects require suitable lab roomsBalanced faculty schedules
Room capacity ≥ enrolled headcountDepartment schedule preferences
Recommendation

For a single-institution SIS, rule-based / assisted scheduling — the system enforces hard constraints and proposes conflict-free assignments while a human (chairperson/registrar) makes the final call — is the practical baseline. Full metaheuristic optimization (genetic algorithms, integer programming) is a legitimate future enhancement, worth adding only once manual scheduling is a measured bottleneck, not a Day-1 requirement.

Part VIII · Grading, Attendance & Academic Progress

Attendance

States: present/absent/late/excused, plus class cancellation and makeup classes. Corrections require an approval workflow and are audited; entries are bound to the scheduled session and timestamped to resist manipulation.

Grading

graph LR A[Faculty enters components] --> B[Submit] B --> C[Dept / Chair Review] C -->|reject| A C -->|approve| D[Registrar Approval] D --> E[Locked / Published] E --> F[Change requires formal request + approval + audit]
Legal requirement

Grades are Sensitive Personal Information under RA 10173 §3(l)(2) — the highest protection tier under the Philippine Data Privacy Act. Direct, unaudited grade edits are both a security anti-pattern and a compliance gap.

Academic progress

Automated degree audit computing completed/remaining/failed subjects, prerequisite deficiencies, GPA/GWA, credits completed, curriculum-completion %, academic standing (Dean's List/probation), and graduation eligibility — with human verification retained for edge cases, mirroring platforms like Stellic and uAchieve (used by 1,800+ institutions).

Part IX · Security & Access Control Research

Standard / regulation
  • OWASP Authorization Cheat Sheet: deny by default, least privilege, per-request server-side checks — Broken Access Control is #1 on the OWASP Top 10.
  • NIST SP 800-63B: favor password length over forced complexity/rotation; screen against breached-password lists.
  • OWASP File Upload, Session Management, and OAuth2 Cheat Sheets for documents, sessions, and API auth respectively.

RESEARCHHigher education is a heavily targeted sector: UpGuard's university security-rating research found software with known exploited vulnerabilities detected at 48% of all universities studied and 70% of the top 500, with phishing and social engineering as the leading threat vector.

RECOMMENDATIONApply the highest protection tier — MFA, encryption at rest and in transit, strict access logging — specifically to grades, financial data, and uploaded documents, since these are the categories both regulation and real-world attackers target first.

Part XI · Database & Data Architecture Research

RESEARCHTemporal/system-versioned tables (per Microsoft's SQL Server temporal-table documentation) and append-only history are the documented alternative to blanket soft-deletion, which is a recognized anti-pattern for anything with legal or historical significance (Cultured Systems; Credera).

Dangerous anti-patterns
  • Hard-deleting historical rows (grades, enrollment, curriculum)
  • Mutating grades or curriculum in place instead of versioning
  • Mixing current and historical state in one mutable row
  • No referential integrity ("God table") or natural keys that can change (name/email as primary key)
  • Hardcoded term values in application code

RECOMMENDATIONCore entities: Student, Faculty, Department, Program, Curriculum, CurriculumVersion, Subject, Section, AcademicYear, Semester, Enrollment, StudentSubject (junction), FacultyAssignment, Room, Schedule, Attendance, Grade, Payment, Document, Notification, AuditLog — with surrogate PKs, FK-enforced integrity, unique constraints (e.g., one active enrollment per student-subject-term), and status columns driving lifecycle state.

Part XII · System Architecture Research

Research finding

Twilio Segment publicly documented migrating away from 140+ microservices back to a consolidated architecture ("Goodbye Microservices"). Amazon Prime Video's engineering team reported that moving a service back to a monolith "reduced our infrastructure cost by over 90%. It also increased our scaling capabilities." Neither team lacked engineering sophistication — both concluded microservices overhead outweighed the benefit for their actual workload.

RECOMMENDATIONBuild Akademus SIS as a modular monolith: one deployable unit with strict internal module boundaries and internal APIs, giving ACID transactions across enrollment/billing (critical for correctness), simpler debugging, lower operating cost, and a clear path to extract a module (e.g., notifications, reporting) into its own service later if it genuinely needs to scale independently.

Part XIV · Philippine Legal & Regulatory Research

Legal requirement
  • RA 10173 (Data Privacy Act of 2012): governs all personal-data processing; academic/education records and grades are Sensitive Personal Information under §3(l)(2), the highest protection class.
  • NPC Circular 16-03: breach notification to the NPC and affected data subjects within 72 hours of knowledge/reasonable belief of a breach, with a full report due within 5 days; failure to notify within 5 days is presumed a violation.
  • Data subject rights (be informed, object, access, rectify, erase/block, port data, claim damages) must be technically implementable in the SIS.
  • CMO No. 40, s.2008 (MORPHE): the omnibus CHED framework for private HEI records, transfer credentials, and honors; Sec. 102 conditions release of credentials on settled accountabilities.
  • CMO No. 20, s.2013: sets the General Education curriculum (~36 units / 8 core courses).
Recommended best practice (not independently mandated)

Data minimization, purpose limitation, privacy-by-design, explicit consent capture, a Privacy Impact Assessment for any analytics/AI feature, and access logging — strongly supportive of DPA compliance, but implemented at institutional discretion rather than dictated line-by-line by law.

CAVEATNo verified, standalone CHED circular fixing national Latin-honors GWA cutoffs or a dedicated records-retention CMO could be confirmed — MORPHE (CMO 40 s.2008) requires each HEI to adopt its own honors policy (Sec. 112). Treat honors thresholds and retention periods as configurable, institution-set values rather than hardcoded legal constants.

Part XV · Performance, Reliability & Recovery

Performance under peak load

Plan explicitly for: enrollment opening, grade-submission deadlines, portal traffic spikes, large reports, schedule generation, and large student searches. Techniques: indexing, query optimization, caching hot counters (e.g., remaining section slots), pagination, and background workers/job queues for anything that isn't instant.

RECOMMENDATIONNever run inside a normal HTTP request: report generation, timetable optimization, bulk notifications, batch transcript generation, or analytics rollups — push these to background jobs.

Backup & disaster recovery

Automated backups with point-in-time recovery, following the 3-2-1 rule (3 copies, 2 media types, 1 offsite). Define RPO (max tolerable data loss) and RTO (max tolerable downtime) per NIST SP 800-34 — suggested targets: RPO 15–60 minutes (daily full + hourly incrementals), RTO a few hours. Restores must be tested regularly — an untested backup is not a backup.

Part XVII · Research Findings

Synthesized conclusions from all preceding research.

Finding 1 — Curriculum and records must be versioned, never overwritten

Evidence: degree-audit vendor documentation (Stellic), temporal-table database guidance (Microsoft), soft-delete anti-pattern writeups (Cultured Systems, Credera). Importance: this is the most common cause of silently corrupted transcripts in real SIS deployments. Development impact: curriculum, enrollment, and grades must be modeled as append-only/temporal from schema Day 1 — retrofitting later requires a full data migration with no guarantee of recovering already-lost history.

Finding 2 — A modular monolith outperforms microservices at this scale

Evidence: Thoughtworks and getDX architecture comparisons; Twilio Segment and Amazon Prime Video's public migrations away from microservices. Importance: avoids unjustified operational cost and complexity for a single-institution system. Development impact: one deployable, one database (with clear internal module boundaries), ACID transactions across enrollment and billing.

Finding 3 — Broken access control is the top real-world risk

Evidence: OWASP Top 10 (Broken Access Control ranked #1); UpGuard's university security research showing widespread known-vulnerability exposure. Importance: student, financial, and grade data are all high-value, high-sensitivity targets. Development impact: deny-by-default, per-request server-side authorization checks on every module — no exceptions for "internal" admin tools.

Finding 4 — Grades and records are legally Sensitive Personal Information in the Philippines

Evidence: RA 10173 §3(l)(2); NPC Circular 16-03's 72-hour breach rule. Importance: converts security and audit-logging practices from best-practice into legal obligation, with real breach-notification deadlines. Development impact: encryption at rest/in transit, full audit trails, and a documented breach-response runbook are launch-blocking, not post-launch improvements.

Finding 5 — Scheduling is provably hard; assisted beats fully automatic at MVP stage

Evidence: university timetabling is a documented NP-hard constraint-satisfaction problem in the academic literature. Importance: full optimization (GA/IP) is real engineering effort with real payoff only past a certain complexity threshold. Development impact: build hard-constraint checking and human-reviewed proposals first; treat automated optimization as a scoped Phase-2+ enhancement.

Part XVIII · Recommendations for Akademus SIS

Concrete engineering decisions translated from the research above.

Model curriculum as versioned, immutable snapshots

CRITICAL
Why
Retrofitting versioning after live data exists risks unrecoverable historical corruption (Finding 1).
Evidence
Part V, Part XI, Finding 1.
Implementation
Program → CurriculumVersion → CurriculumSubject; every student assigned to one version at admission; version migrations are explicit, approved, audited events.
Risk if ignored
Silent, retroactive breakage of past students' degree audits when curricula change.

Build as a modular monolith

CRITICAL
Why
ACID transactions across enrollment/billing, lower operating cost, simpler debugging for a small team building for one institution (Finding 2).
Evidence
Part XII.
Implementation
One deployable app; enforce module boundaries at the code-organization level (e.g., separate packages/namespaces per domain) even though it's one process.
Risk if ignored
Distributed-system complexity (eventual consistency, service discovery) with no corresponding scale benefit.

Deny-by-default RBAC with per-request server-side authorization

CRITICAL
Why
Broken access control is the top real-world SIS risk (Finding 3).
Evidence
Part IV, Part IX.
Implementation
Central authorization middleware checked on every route/handler; role-and-scope model (department, advisee-list) not just role name.
Risk if ignored
Any user able to view or modify data outside their scope — a Data Privacy Act violation as well as a security failure.

Full audit trail with append-only storage

CRITICAL
Why
Required for override accountability, grade-change traceability, and DPA-aligned data-subject transparency.
Evidence
Part X.
Implementation
Separate audit store or append-only table; capture actor, action, entity, before/after, reason, timestamp on every write to sensitive domains.
Risk if ignored
No accountability for grade fraud, unauthorized overrides, or breach investigation.

Enforce concurrency safety on enrollment and room booking

HIGH
Why
Last-seat and room-double-booking races are common, well-documented failure modes.
Evidence
Part XI database research; optimistic/pessimistic locking literature.
Implementation
Database transactions + unique constraints as the backstop; pessimistic locking (SELECT ... FOR UPDATE) on last-seat enrollment specifically.
Risk if ignored
Oversold sections, double-booked rooms, corrupted enrollment counts.

Grade workflow: enter → submit → review → approve → lock

HIGH
Why
Separation of duties prevents unilateral grade fraud; grades are legally SPI.
Evidence
Part VIII.
Implementation
Locked grades editable only via a formal change-request workflow with second approver and audit entry.
Risk if ignored
Unaudited grade tampering; loss of institutional trust in the transcript.

Assisted (not fully automatic) scheduling and sectioning

MEDIUM
Why
Timetabling is NP-hard; full optimization is expensive to build correctly and isn't needed at MVP scale (Finding 5).
Evidence
Part VII, Part IX (section management).
Implementation
System enforces hard constraints and proposes assignments; chairperson/registrar approves. Defer GA/IP optimization to a later phase if manual review becomes a bottleneck.
Risk if ignored (over-building)
Significant early engineering effort spent on optimization the institution doesn't yet need.

Delegate heavy accounting to a dedicated finance system

MEDIUM
Why
Keeps SIS scope focused on academic-financial assessment rather than reimplementing a general ledger.
Evidence
Part IV (billing module), Part XIII (integration).
Implementation
SIS owns assessment tied to enrollment; nightly reconciliation job against ERP; payments routed through a gateway, never storing card data.
Risk if ignored
Duplicated, conflicting financial records between systems.

Document a breach-response runbook aligned to the 72-hour NPC rule

HIGH
Why
Legal deadline, not best practice — failure to notify within 5 days is presumed a violation.
Evidence
Part XIV; NPC Circular 16-03.
Implementation
Predefined breach-classification criteria, notification templates, and an on-call escalation path, tested before go-live.
Risk if ignored
Regulatory penalty and reputational damage compounding an already-bad incident.

Part XXII · Recommended Development Roadmap

Phase 1 — Foundation

Modules: Identity/RBAC, audit logging, academic structure, system configuration, term management. Dependencies: none. Risks: weak permissions here are expensive to retrofit. Definition of Done: deny-by-default authorization tested; every write audited; no hardcoded terms anywhere in code.

Phase 2 — Academic Core

Modules: curriculum versioning, subjects/prerequisites, student records, program/curriculum assignment. Dependencies: Phase 1. Definition of Done: two curriculum versions coexist correctly; historical-integrity regression tests pass.

Phase 3 — Enrollment

Modules: sections, enrollment workflow, validations, concurrency handling, billing assessment. Definition of Done: last-slot race-condition test passes; all business rules enforced server-side.

Phase 4 — Scheduling

Modules: rooms, assisted scheduling, faculty assignment, conflict detection. Definition of Done: no hard-constraint violation is publishable.

Phase 5 — Academic Operations

Modules: attendance, grading workflow with locking and change-audit. Definition of Done: locked grades immutable outside the approved change workflow.

Phase 6 — Supporting Services

Modules: documents (OWASP-aligned), notifications, clearance, portal polish. Definition of Done: file-upload validation and download authorization both pass security tests.

Phase 7 — Graduation & Reporting

Modules: degree audit, graduation eligibility with human verification, reports/analytics as background jobs. Definition of Done: graduation cannot be conferred without registrar sign-off.

Phase 8 — Production Readiness

Modules: backups with tested restores, defined RPO/RTO, load testing, security testing, monitoring, documented breach-response runbook. Definition of Done: a restore drill has actually been performed, not just scheduled.

Part XXIV · Top 20 Recommendations

  1. Deny-by-default RBAC, checked server-side on every request — the single most common real-world SIS failure is here.
  2. Immutable/versioned academic records — never hard-delete or overwrite curriculum, enrollment, or grade history.
  3. Curriculum versioning with per-student version assignment.
  4. Stable surrogate student identity, deduplicated at admission time.
  5. Transactions + locking + unique constraints for enrollment and room-booking concurrency.
  6. Comprehensive, tamper-evident audit logging on every sensitive-domain write.
  7. Grade workflow with locking and audited change requests — never a direct edit.
  8. Server-side enforcement of every business rule — the frontend is not a security boundary.
  9. Encrypt Sensitive Personal Information at rest and in transit; manage secrets properly.
  10. MFA for privileged roles; NIST-aligned password policy.
  11. OWASP-aligned file-upload handling; authorize every document download individually.
  12. Configuration as data — terms, curricula, fee tables, honors thresholds — never hardcoded.
  13. Explicit integration source-of-truth boundaries with idempotent, retriable events.
  14. Automated degree audit with mandatory human verification before graduation.
  15. Background jobs for anything non-instant — reports, notifications, optimization.
  16. Tested backups with defined RPO/RTO — an untested backup is not a backup.
  17. Documented, drilled breach-response process aligned to the NPC's 72-hour rule.
  18. Separation of duties — the grade enterer is never the sole approver/locker.
  19. Safe, logged, approved override workflow, with clearly non-overridable rules enforced in code.
  20. Modular monolith over premature microservices for this scale of institution.

Part XXVI · Final Conclusion

If we were building a modern, secure, scalable, maintainable, auditable, production-ready Student Information System for a real college or university from scratch today, how should it be designed?

Build it as a modular monolith with strict internal domain boundaries and ACID transactions, on a relational database using versioned/temporal patterns for all academic history. Get the student identity model, curriculum versioning, and record-immutability decisions right in Phase 1 — they are the most expensive to reverse later. Enforce deny-by-default RBAC with separation of duties and a tamper-evident audit log, because grades and academic records are legally Sensitive Personal Information under the Philippine Data Privacy Act. Automate validations and degree audits, but keep humans in the loop for overrides, grade locking, sectioning, and graduation conferral. Use assisted, constraint-checked scheduling rather than full optimization at MVP stage. Integrate with Admissions, the LMS, and accounting through clear source-of-truth boundaries and idempotent events. Harden per OWASP/NIST/ISO 27001, and back the whole system with tested point-in-time recovery and a documented breach-response runbook aligned to the NPC's 72-hour rule.
