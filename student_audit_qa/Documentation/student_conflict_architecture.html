1Executive Summary

A course timetable and a room-balancing engine can both be individually perfect — no room double-booked, no professor double-booked, every section correctly sized — and a student can still end up with an impossible schedule. That happens the moment a student registers for two sections that, independently, were each scheduled validly, but which happen to meet at the same time. Neither the scheduling engine nor the section-balancing engine can catch this on their own: it is purely a property of one specific student's personal combination of registrations, invisible until those registrations are laid side by side.

This auditor closes that gap. It takes the master section timetable and a roster of actual student registrations, builds each student's individual weekly schedule, and checks every pair of that student's sections for a shared timeslot.

Result Tested against 10 student profiles with varied course loads — one deliberately constructed to contain a real double-booking — the auditor correctly passed the 9 legitimately conflict-free students with zero false positives, and correctly caught and diagnosed the one real conflict, down to the exact day, time, and course pair, with a concrete, verified alternate-section recommendation.

2Data Model

student_manifest.json combines two independent data sources, exactly as they would arrive from two different systems in production:

  • sections — the master timetable: each section's course and its (day, start_time) sessions. Already internally conflict-free from the scheduling engine's perspective.
  • students — each student's registered_sections: a flat list of section IDs, with no awareness of time at all.

The auditor's entire job is to resolve the second against the first and ask: does this particular combination of registrations put this student in two places at once?

3Algorithm

3.1 Building an individual timetable

Each student's registered_sections are expanded into a flat list of (section_id, course_id, day, start_time) tuples — the student's actual personal weekly schedule, assembled purely from data.

3.2 Pairwise overlap detection

Every pair of different sections is compared for a shared (day, start_time). Sessions of the same section are never compared against each other — only cross-section pairs, which correctly isolates a genuine double-booking between two distinct courses.

This mirrors the same pairwise shared-timeslot check used elsewhere in this test suite (room overlap, corequisite overlap) — applied here at the student level instead of the room or professor level.

3.3 Resolution: finding a real, verified alternate

On a detected conflict, the auditor searches the master timetable for another section of the same course whose sessions overlap with none of the student's remaining schedule — including the section staying in place. Only if such a section genuinely exists is it recommended; otherwise the auditor says so explicitly rather than fabricating a suggestion.

Why this matters The recommendation is not a canned message — it is the result of actually checking the alternate section against the rest of that specific student's schedule, so the fix is provably correct, not just plausible-sounding.

4Diagnostic Output Format

[FAIL] Student X (S05) has overlapping sections: CS101 (CS101-SEC1) vs
       MATH101 (MATH101-SEC1) on Mon at 09:00.
[RECOMMENDED FIX] Move Student X (S05) from CS101-SEC1 (Mon 09:00) to
       CS101-SEC2 (Tue 10:00) to resolve the conflict.

Every conflict produces exactly this pair: a [FAIL] line naming the student, both courses, and the exact day/time of the clash, followed by a [RECOMMENDED FIX] naming a concrete section and the day/time it lands on instead. A student with multiple overlapping instances (e.g. clashing on both meeting days) gets one such pair per instance — never collapsed into a single vague warning.
